Which bid wins the ticket-triage pilot?

Give both firms the same held-back tickets and ask one of your operators to trace a misroute using each firm’s handover notes. These fictional bids illustrate how to record what the panel saw.

Choose weights before inviting bids

For a pilot with supplier support, the example weights below give delivery 45%, handover 35% and commercial clarity 20%. Bid A earns 82/100 (45 + 21 + 16). Bid B earns 78/100 (27 + 35 + 16). The panel buys better routing but must budget staff time for assisted recovery.

For a different RFP where your team takes over on day one, giving handover 45% and delivery 35% reverses the result. B earns 82/100 and A earns 78/100. B’s handover advantage comes with two routing errors. Ask who will catch those tickets and how much rework they cause. Keep the published weights throughout each competition.

What goes in the award memo?

If buyer-owned repositories are mandatory and A has only promised access, record “A: 82, pending repository evidence.” Name the reviewer and the evidence needed to clear the condition. Record B’s eligibility separately. Ask each finalist for its missing price line before comparing total cost. A commercial clarity score measures how well the quote explains costs, not how low the price is.
