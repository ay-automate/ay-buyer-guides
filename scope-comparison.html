What does the quoted fee buy?

The UK Service Manual separates discovery, where a team investigates the problem, from alpha, where it tests possible solutions. Here is how a finance team could apply that distinction to invoice matching. The fees and hours are fictional.

Discovery · decide whether to fund a prototype

For a £6,000 fee, the consultant reviews disputed invoices with accounts payable. Your staff contribute 12 hours at £50 an hour. Budget £6,600 in total.

Before sign-off Ask the finance lead to show which exceptions consume the most time and which sample invoices the team can use to test a fix. Read the Service Manual on discovery.

Prototype · test a risky assumption

For a £9,000 fee, the consultant tests whether line items still match when a supplier splits a delivery. A clerk spends 20 hours preparing and reviewing cases at £50 an hour. Budget £10,000 in total.

Before sign-off Have the clerk replay held-back invoices and count correct matches, false matches and referrals. Ask for a separate price for production integration. Read the Service Manual on alpha.

Record the quote you would approve

Ask each bidder to define a unit, list units by role and phase, and quote a capped fee. Add your team’s hours and usage charges. Compare totals only for bids that deliver the same phase.
